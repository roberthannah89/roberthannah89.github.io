The Swiss trail system

Switzerland's 65,000 km hiking network is constitutionally protected, federally funded, and systematically organised.

Trail classifications

Every marked trail in Switzerland falls into one of three legally defined categories, each with consistent signposting across the entire country.

The SAC hiking scale (T1–T6)

GradeMarkingTerrainFootwearRequirementsExamples
T1
Hiking		 Well-marked path, level or gently sloping Walking shoes Basic fitness
T2
Mountain hiking		 / Uneven or rocky sections, some steep stretches. No exposure. Hiking shoes with ankle support Sure footing, good basic fitness Doldenhornhuette Sac, Faulhorn
T3
Demanding mountain hiking		 Partly absent paths. Exposed sections possible, hands occasionally needed. Fixed ropes/chains on worst sections. Mountain boots Surefootedness, no vertigo Hörnlihütte, Oberaletschhütte SAC
T4
Alpine hiking		 Trails partly absent. Exposed ridges, hands needed. Steep scree or loose rock. Stiff mountain boots Surefootedness, freedom from vertigo, alpine experience. Start early (thunderstorm risk). Augstmatthorn, Bettmerhorn
T5
Demanding alpine hiking		 No path. Glaciers, snowfields, technical rock. Sustained exposure. Mountaineering boots Crampons, ice axe, rope skills. Self-rescue capability.
T6
Difficult alpine hiking		 Technical climbing terrain. Sustained technical difficulty. Climbing equipment Alpine climbing experience (rock and ice). Rope required.

T1–T3 is accessible to any reasonably fit person with decent footwear. T4 is where it starts feeling committing — real exposure, consequences for a slip. T5–T6 are for experienced alpinists only.

See the official SAC hiking scale description for the authoritative definitions.

Route hierarchy

SchweizMobil organises signposted routes into three tiers, identified by their route number on green stickers at trailheads and junctions.

SignTierScope
National (single digit) Long-distance trails crossing the country. 7 routes, typically 15–39 stages.
Regional (two digits) Multi-stage routes crossing cantonal boundaries. Often 3–10 stages.
Local (three digits) Day hikes within a single region. The bulk of the network.

Via Alpina Vaduz → Montreux 20 The classic alpine pass route. 14 passes, hut-to-hut.
