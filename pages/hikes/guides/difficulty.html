Trail grades: T1–T6

The SAC (Swiss Alpine Club) mountain hiking scale is the standard for Swiss trails. Here's what each grade actually feels like, and what you need to be prepared for.

In Switzerland, the trail markings usually give you the first hint: yellow signs are ordinary hiking, white-red-white markings indicate mountain hiking, and white-blue-white markings indicate alpine hiking. The SAC grade and the sign colour usually line up.

My take: T1–T3 is accessible to any reasonably fit person with decent footwear. T4 is when things start to feel committing — exposure is real, a slip has consequences, and you need to be comfortable with heights. T5–T6 are for experienced alpinists only.

T1 — Hiking

Typical signposting: Yellow signs and yellow diamond-shaped blazes.

Terrain: Well-marked paths on level or gently sloping terrain. No obstacles.

  • Walking shoes or trail runners are fine.
  • No special fitness required — accessible to all ages.
  • Examples: valley walks, lakeside paths, forest trails.

T2 — Mountain hiking

Typical signposting: Often still yellow signposts, but mountain terrain begins to appear. Some routes transition into white-red-white markings.

Terrain: Paths with some uneven or rocky sections. Possible short steep stretches. Marked trails throughout.

  • Hiking shoes with ankle support recommended (trail runners work for experienced hikers).
  • Good basic fitness — some sustained ascent.
  • No exposure to drops. Suitable for beginners who are comfortable on uneven ground.
  • My view: This is a pleasant, achievable challenge for most people. Berghotel Faulhorn and Europahütte are good T2 examples.

T3 — Demanding mountain hiking

Typical signposting: White-red-white trail markers and pointer tips on signposts.

Terrain: Paths may be partly absent. Exposed sections possible. Hands may be needed for balance occasionally.

  • Mountain boots with a stiff sole are recommended. Trail runners feel insecure on wet rock.
  • Good fitness and surefootedness required.
  • Short sections of exposure where a fall could have serious consequences — but fixed ropes or chains are usually present on the worst sections.
  • Experience reading terrain and route-finding in mist is useful.
  • My view: T3 is where it starts feeling like an alpine hike rather than a walk. Berggasthaus Rotsteinpass and Monte Tamaro are T3 routes with some brief exposed sections.

T4 — Alpine hiking

Typical signposting: White-blue-white markers on rock and blue-tipped route signs.

Terrain: Trails partly absent or require route-finding. Exposed ridges. Hands definitely needed on some sections. Steep scree or loose rock possible.

  • Mountain boots with a stiff sole are required — trail runners are not safe here.
  • Surefootedness and freedom from vertigo are essential.
  • Experience with exposed alpine terrain strongly recommended. If you're not comfortable with sustained exposure to drops, don't attempt T4.
  • Poles are useful for descent on loose terrain.
  • Early start recommended — afternoon thunderstorm risk and avoiding crowds on narrow ridges.
  • My view: T4 feels committing. Route-finding errors are possible. Augstmatthorn and Bettmerhorn are T4 routes that feel serious when conditions are less than perfect.

T5 — Demanding alpine hiking

Typical signposting: White-blue-white or no clear path at all once you are on the route.

Terrain: Paths absent. Glaciers, snowfields, or highly technical rocky terrain. Sustained exposure. Navigation requires experience.

  • Crampons and ice axe may be required depending on season.
  • Rope and basic climbing skills may be needed.
  • Only for experienced alpinists with good mountain judgment.
  • Self-rescue capability required — rescue in this terrain is slow and difficult.
  • A qualified mountain guide is strongly recommended unless you have extensive alpine experience.

T6 — Difficult alpine hiking

Typical signposting: White-blue-white where signposted, but route-finding and alpine judgment matter far more than paint.

Terrain: Demanding alpine routes requiring climbing experience. Very exposed. Technical difficulty comparable to easy climbing routes.

  • Rope and climbing equipment necessary.
  • Requires alpine climbing experience (rock and ice).
  • Beyond the scope of this guide — hire a guide from the SAC or a certified mountain guiding company.
