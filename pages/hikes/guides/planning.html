Planning a hike

How to estimate time, get to the trailhead, plan bail-outs, and avoid the afternoon thunderstorm window. Swiss-specific logistics that determine whether a day goes well or badly.

Signpost times (Wanderzeit)

The times on yellow Swiss signposts are calculated using a standard formula maintained by swisstopo. They assume no breaks and good conditions.

Time = (distance_km × 15 min) + (ascent_m / 100 × 15 min) + (descent_m / 200 × 15 min)

Which implies:

ComponentPace
Horizontal4.2 km/h
Ascent400 m/h
Descent800 m/h

How much buffer to add

Fitness levelBufferNotes
Fit, experienced hiker+10–20%Covers rest stops, photos, navigation. Signpost times are achievable but leave no margin.
Average fitness+30–40%Standard for occasional hikers. A 5h signpost route becomes 6.5–7h.
Beginner or group+50%Groups move at the slowest member's pace. Beginners underestimate descent time.
Common mistake: signpost times don't include breaks. A "6-hour" hike with a lunch stop, two water breaks, and some photography is realistically 7.5–8 hours door-to-door.

The early start

Experienced Swiss mountain hikers start at 05:00–06:00. This isn't machismo — it's thunderstorm risk management.

Why it matters

  • Summer thunderstorms build from late morning, with cells developing 13:00–16:00.
  • Lightning above treeline is the second deadliest natural hazard in Switzerland.
  • Snow/ice crossings are firmer in the morning (less rockfall, safer glacier surfaces).
  • Visibility deteriorates through the day as haze builds.

Target timeline (6–8 hour summer hike)

TimeMilestone
05:00–06:00Depart trailhead
09:00–11:00Reach summit or highest exposed point
11:00–13:00Begin descent
13:00–15:00Below exposed terrain before storm window

If public transport doesn't reach the trailhead before 07:00, consider staying at a valley hotel or SAC hut the night before. First mountain PostBuses typically depart 06:30–08:00.

Public transport

Almost every trailhead in Switzerland is reachable by public transport. Use the SBB app — it covers trains, PostBus, boats, and many cable cars in one ticket.

Ticket economics

OptionCostBest for
Halbtax CHF 190/year Anyone hiking 4+ times/year. 50% off everything including cable cars.
GA Travelcard CHF 3,995/year (2nd class) Weekly hikers. Unlimited travel including most mountain railways.
Saver Day Pass + Halbtax from CHF 29/day One-off full-day trips. Can sell out — book ahead.
Supersaver (Sparbillett) up to 50% off Fixed to one specific connection. Non-refundable. Good for known early trains.

PostBus specifics

  • Seasonal routes: many Alpine PostBus lines run June–October only.
  • Last departures: mountain PostBus routes often have last buses at 16:30–18:30. Always check the specific route — missing the last bus means a taxi or a long walk.
  • Seat reservations: recommended for popular pass routes (Furka, Grimsel, Susten). Reserve by 16:00 the day before via postauto.ch or 0848 100 222.
  • Groups of 10+: reservation mandatory.

Cable cars

  • Most accept Halbtax (50% discount) and GA (free or discounted).
  • Typical first ascent: 08:00–08:30. Last descent: 16:30–17:30.
  • Operating seasons vary — some close mid-October, others run year-round. Always verify.

Bail-out planning

Switzerland's trail network is 65,000+ km with 50,000 signposts — one of the densest in the world. This means bail-out options almost always exist. Identify them before you start.

Before leaving, identify:

  • Trail junctions with paths descending to valleys — these are your decision points.
  • Cable car or gondola stations that could shorten your return.
  • PostBus stops on valley roads below your route (and their last departure times).
  • SAC huts as emergency shelter, even if you don't plan to stay.
  • Time to each bail-out using the signpost formula — know how long retreat takes from each point.

When to bail

  • Weather deteriorating (see weather guide for go/no-go criteria).
  • Significantly behind schedule (>50% slower than signpost times).
  • Any member of the group showing signs of exhaustion, vertigo, or distress.
  • Trail conditions worse than expected (snow, ice, wet rock making the grade effectively higher).
  • Visibility dropping below ~50 m in unfamiliar terrain.

Grade progression rules

  • Never jump from T2 to T4. T3 is where you learn whether exposure and scrambling suit you.
  • Grades assume dry conditions and good visibility. Rain effectively raises difficulty by 1–2 grades.
  • Start a new grade on a short route (3–4h) before committing to a full day.
  • Comfort on descent matters more than on ascent — most accidents happen going down.

Warning signs you're in over your head

  • Needing both hands constantly on a trail rated below T4.
  • Feeling dizzy or anxious at exposed sections.
  • Consistently 50%+ slower than signpost pace.
  • Exhaustion before reaching the halfway point.
  • Unable to identify the trail markings or route ahead.

Before you leave

  • Tell someone: route, expected return time, and the time at which they should worry. Always for solo T3+, always for routes over 6 hours.
  • Rega app: install it and enable activity monitoring — if you fail to check in, it alerts your emergency contacts.
  • Offline maps: download the region in swisstopo. Cell coverage above 1,500 m is unreliable.
  • Check weather: MeteoSwiss app the evening before and again at 06:00. See the weather guide.
  • Check trail status: SchweizMobil shows current closures and detours.
  • Charge your phone: GPS navigation drains battery fast. Consider a power bank for hikes over 6 hours.
