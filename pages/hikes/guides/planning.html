Planning a hike

The Swiss hiking infrastructure is exceptional. These three tools are all you need to find a route, build a navigation file, and get to the trailhead by public transport.

SAC Route Portal

The Swiss Alpine Club (SAC) maintains the definitive database of Swiss alpine routes. It has over 6,000 curated routes covering mountain hiking, alpine hiking, ski touring, snowshoeing, and climbing.

Finding a route

  • Filter by activity type, difficulty (T1–T6 or I–VI for climbing), duration, and region.
  • Each route shows elevation gain/loss, estimated time, waypoints, and hazard warnings.
  • The interactive map uses SwissTopo as the base layer — the most detailed topographic map of Switzerland.
  • On the map: red trails are T1–T3 (mountain hiking), blue trails are T4–T6 (alpine hiking). Red houses are SAC huts. Green peaks are hiking peaks; purple are alpine peaks.

Reading a peak's routes

  1. Click a green peak icon on the map.
  2. You'll see a list of documented routes with their difficulty. "Archived routes" are old routes from historical hiking texts — treat them with extra care.
  3. Click a route to get the full description (usually in German — translate with the browser or DeepL), waypoints, and time estimates. The time estimates are accurate for a fit individual.
  4. Look for the public transport link — it shows you which train/bus combination gets you to the trailhead.

Downloading GPX files

Most SAC routes have a downloadable GPX file. Save this to your phone before the hike — cell reception is unreliable in the mountains. Import it into the SwissTopo app or Komoot for offline navigation.

SAC huts

Switzerland has 153 SAC huts integrated into the portal. For multi-day hikes, you can plan a route that strings huts together and book accommodation in advance through the SAC website.

Komoot

Komoot is the best tool for building your own routes rather than relying on pre-defined SAC routes. It has a web version and a mobile app (linked to Strava).

What it's good for

  • Custom route planning: Draw a route on the map and Komoot snaps it to hiking trails, shows elevation profile, and estimates time at each segment.
  • Steepness and grade per segment: It shows the SAC trail rating and gradient at every point in the route — invaluable for judging technical sections in advance.
  • User photos: Community-contributed photos at specific locations along the route give you a realistic preview of the terrain and views.
  • Navigation: Turn-by-turn navigation on mobile, works offline with downloaded maps.
  • GPX export: Export any route as a GPX file to use in SwissTopo or other apps.

Saved hike plans

I keep dozens of planned hikes in Komoot. It's a good way to build a wishlist — you can plan a hike months in advance, check the route details, and then pull it up when you're ready to go.

SwissTopo app

The SwissTopo app gives you access to Switzerland's official 1:25,000 topographic maps on your phone. These are the most detailed and accurate maps available for Swiss terrain.

  • Download maps for offline use before you leave — cell coverage is unreliable above 1,500 m.
  • You can overlay hiking trails, SAC hut locations, and your own GPX tracks.
  • The app shows your real-time GPS position on the topo map.
  • For T4+ hikes where navigation judgment matters, SwissTopo on a downloaded topo is essential.

Getting to the trailhead

Switzerland's public transport is world-class. Almost every trailhead is reachable by a combination of train, PostBus, and sometimes gondola/cable car. Plan your transit at SBB (Swiss Federal Railways) or the SBB app.

  • The SAC Route Portal links directly to an SBB transit query for each route's trailhead.
  • PostBus (PostAuto) covers many mountain valleys — buy a ticket in the SBB app, it includes PostBus routes.
  • The Swiss Travel Pass / GA covers trains, PostBus, and many gondolas — highly worth it if you hike regularly.
  • Gondolas and cable cars often have a reduced-price first/last-journey ticket if you only take it one way.
