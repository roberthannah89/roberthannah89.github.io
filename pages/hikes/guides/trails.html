The hiking network

Switzerland's 65,000 km hiking network is constitutionally protected, federally funded, and systematically organised. This page explains who maintains the trails, how routes are numbered, and what the 7 national long-distance routes are.

Constitutional protection

Switzerland is one of the few countries where hiking trails are protected by the national constitution. In February 1979, voters approved a popular initiative with 77.6% support. The provision now appears as Article 88 of the Federal Constitution, implemented through the Federal Act on Footpaths and Hiking Trails (1985). If a trail is removed for development, the canton must provide an adequate replacement.

Who maintains the trails

OrganisationRole
SR Suisse Rando National hiking federation (est. 1934). Coordinates trail standards and signposting across 26 cantonal organisations. ~2,000 volunteers maintain the network.
KT Cantonal organisations Each canton plans, finances, and signposts its trails. Most delegate construction and upkeep to municipalities.
SM SchweizMobil Federal foundation operating the Wanderland platform — the official trail map, route database, and GPS tracks.
SAC Swiss Alpine Club 6,000+ curated alpine routes, 153 mountain huts, and the T1–T6 difficulty scale. See Planning.
ST swisstopo Federal Office of Topography — official 1:25,000 maps and the SwissTopo app.

Route hierarchy

SchweizMobil organises signposted routes into three tiers, identified by their route number on green stickers at trailheads and junctions.

SignTierScope
1 National (single digit) Long-distance trails crossing the country. 7 routes, typically 15–39 stages.
55 Regional (two digits) Multi-stage routes crossing cantonal boundaries. Often 3–10 stages.
305 Local (three digits) Day hikes within a single region. The bulk of the network.

The 7 national routes

RouteFrom → ToStagesCharacter
1 Via Alpina Vaduz → Montreux 20 The classic alpine pass route. 14 passes, hut-to-hut.
2 Trans Swiss Trail Porrentruy → Mendrisio 32 North-to-south traverse (488 km). Every landscape and language.
3 Alpine Panorama Trail Bodensee → Lac Léman ~28 East-to-west along the Alpine foothills. Constant panorama.
4 ViaJacobi Bodensee → Genève ~19 Way of St. James. Churches, monasteries, cultural heritage.
5 Jura Crest Trail Zürich → Genève ~15 The gentle long-distance hike. Rolling Jura ridgelines.
6 Alpine Passes Trail Chur → Lac Léman 39 Most demanding national route. 30 passes, full alpine commitment.
7 ViaGottardo Basel → Chiasso ~20 Historic approaches to the Gotthard — the crossing that shaped Switzerland.

All national routes have downloadable GPX tracks on SchweizMobil. Individual stages can be hiked as standalone day hikes. For curated day-hike selections, see the SchweizMobil Top 32 Walks or this site's 50 Golden Classics.
