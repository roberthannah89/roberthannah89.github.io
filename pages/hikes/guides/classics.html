50 Golden Classics

A curated list of the 50 essential Swiss hikes — the routes that define hiking in Switzerland, ranked roughly by fame. These span every major region and difficulty level, from gentle lakeside walks to committing alpine ridges.

★ = route page on this site

24 of 50 classics have route pages on this site.

# Hike Grade Region Description
1 Höhenweg Hohbalm T2 Wallis Zermatt panorama trail beneath the Matterhorn — arguably Switzerland's most iconic vista.
2 Schynige Platte – First T2 Berner Oberland The definitive Oberland ridge traverse with Eiger, Mönch, and Jungfrau in your face the entire way.
3 Aletsch Glacier panorama trail T2 Wallis Walk along the rim of the longest glacier in the Alps from Bettmerhorn or Eggishorn.
4 Oeschinensee T2 Berner Oberland UNESCO World Heritage lake set in a dramatic limestone cirque above Kandersteg.
5 ★Rigi Kulm T2 Zentralschweiz The Queen of the Mountains — panoramic summit above the lake, reachable by rack railway or on foot.
6 ★Pilatus (Tomlishorn) T4 Zentralschweiz The dragon mountain above Lucerne — exposed summit ridge with chains and a legendary history.
7 Lauterbrunnen – Mürren T1 Berner Oberland Valley of 72 waterfalls to the car-free village, with Staubbach Falls and Jungfrau views.
8 Bachalpsee T1 Berner Oberland Short walk from First gondola to a mirror-still lake reflecting the Schreckhorn and Wetterhorn.
9 Europaweg (Grächen – Zermatt) T3 Wallis Two-day high trail with the Charles Kuonen suspension bridge and Matterhorn views.
10 5-Seenweg (Pizol) T2 Ostschweiz Five glacial lakes in a single circuit above Bad Ragaz — from turquoise to emerald.
11 ★Säntis T3 Alpstein Highest Alpstein summit with 360° panorama across six countries.
12 Hardergrat T4 Berner Oberland Legendary airy ridge from Interlaken to Brienz — one of Switzerland's most exposed day hikes.

38 Cardada – Cimetta T1 Ticino Above Locarno — panoramic terrace over Lago Maggiore to the snow peaks.

Selection criteria

These 50 routes were chosen for their combination of scenic quality, cultural significance, variety of terrain, and accessibility by public transport. The list balances regions (Oberland, Wallis, Zentralschweiz, Ostschweiz, Graubünden, Ticino, Jura, Romandie) and difficulty levels (T1–T5) so that every hiker finds something.

This is a personal selection, not an official ranking. If you think a classic is missing, you're probably right — Switzerland has too many great hikes for any list of 50.
