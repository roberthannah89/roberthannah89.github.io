Rigi Kulm 1797 m

SAC T5- · ~980 m gain · ~11.9 km one-way · ~8h round-trip

In a description from 1479 the deacon of the Einsiedeln monastery called Rigi «mons regina». In later texts Rigena, Riginun or Rigam are mentioned. They are all derived from «Riga», Latin for „line“, „trait“, „rock belt“, or „fold“. Scientists of today see the origin of the name Rigi in the „Rigenen“, those clearly distinguishable rock belts on the north and west sides of Rigi Kulm. Rigi is undisputedly an attractive mountain for alpine hiking. Proof are the ascent and especially the descent, which are far away from the daily hubbub of mass tourism.

Rigi Kulm is the highest point of the massif, made accessible almost to the very top by the famous Rigi railway. There are secluded, adventurous paths on the precipitous north and west faces, while the south and east faces offer gentle trails for casual strolls.

Trip Reports (hikr.org)

Key takeaways

  • Nagelfluh slipperiness: Nearly every report warns that the Nagelfluh conglomerate rock becomes extremely slippery when wet; mud on boots compounds the problem, reducing footholds to near-zero.
  • Exposure on Arschbaggen: The Arschbaggen section involves ~15 m of fixed-cable climbing on steep conglomerate; considerable arm strength is required as natural footholds are scarce.
  • Contrast with tourist Rigi: The north and west flanks are remote and serious, a world apart from the crowded railway summit — but expect crowds at Rigi Kulm itself.

Recent reports

Abenteuer Rigi Nord · June 2023 · T5-
  • Ascent via Arschbaggen, descent via Leiterliweg with many ladders and secured passages on the NE flank.
  • Rated T5-II for the climbing sections; the Leiterliweg descent is somewhat easier (T4) but overgrown in places.
  • Steep, rooted path from Ronenboden leads into short conglomerate climbing sections before the main Arschbaggen crux.
Rigi Kulm über Füdlibaggen · July 2022 · T5-
  • Ascent from Seebodenalp (07:55 start) via the Füdlibaggen/Arschbaggen variant to the summit.
  • Emphasises the fixed steel-cable sections on Nagelfluh rock and the need for dry conditions.
  • Route finding on the upper sections requires attention; faint paths through conglomerate bands.
Rigi: Aufstieg über den Tristenboden · September 2020 · T5-
  • Ascent from Seebodenalp via Ronenboden, Arschbagge, and Tristenboden to Rigi Kulm.
  • Clay on boots made the Nagelfluh extremely difficult; significant arm strength needed on the fixed ropes.
  • Done during COVID railway closures — Seebodenalp was a popular alternative start, but the north side remained quiet.
