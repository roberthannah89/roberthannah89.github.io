Sailing list for Miami

What to bring sailing in Miami — and why.

ItemWhatWhy
Weather check Check the HRRR model on Windy and talk to staff. Critical. Check every time and ask staff what conditions are. I use windy.com — specifically the HRRR weather model for accurate, high-resolution short-term forecasts.
Sunglasses Polarized sunglasses. Extremely bright when sailing — direct sun plus reflection from the water. Polarized lenses block reflected light. Any sunglasses are better than none.
Sunscreen Sports sunscreen, high SPF. Needs to be water-resistant. Reapply every 80 minutes. I use Banana Boat Sport Ultra SPF 65: link.
Sandals / shoes Sandals or wet shoes. Don't technically need shoes. Sandals should be sufficient. Barefoot is also fine — but there is coral you have to walk over to reach the boat in Miami.
Clothing Long-sleeve rash shirt; swimming shorts or pants; soft shell when colder or windier. Unless you're on a yacht, assume you'll get wet. Even on a warm day, water + wind evaporation can get cold. Synthetic material retains warmth when wet — long-sleeve synthetic rash shirts are best for warmth and sun protection. When it's colder or windier, a rash shirt may not be enough — bring a rain jacket or soft shell. If you get cold easily, bring more than you need.
Water 1 L per hour. If you're thirsty you won't have fun. Bring more than you need — someone will forget and you'll have to share.
Phone protection Waterproof phone bag. Salt water destroys the rubber seals that keep your phone waterproof — IPX rating doesn't matter. I use the Pelican Marine IP68 Waterproof Phone Pouch: link.
Dry clothing & towel Optional waterproof bag for your dry stuff. Leave your dry stuff on shore — keep it away from sand and water.
