Bächistock (Glärnisch) 2914 m

Photo via hikr.org - Glärnisch and Bächistock

The Glärnisch massif dominates the Glarus skyline — a broad glaciated fortress rising above the Linth valley that has defined the identity of the canton since the Ice Age. Its highest point, Bächistock (2914 m), is reached by a superb T3 day-hike from Braunwald, a uniquely charming car-free alpine resort perched above Linthal and reachable only by funicular.
— Route compiled from SAC data, hikr.org, and local sources.

The Glärnisch is the largest glacier massif in the Glarus Alps, with three main summits: Bächistock (2914 m), Vrenelisgärtli (2904 m), and Ruchen (2901 m). The massif's glacier is retreating visibly — the ice coverage has shrunk dramatically since the 19th century — making the upper plateau an increasingly rocky landscape that only emphasises the raw scale of the terrain.

The classic hiking route ascends from Braunwald (1256 m) via the jewel-green Oberblegisee (1755 m), one of the finest mountain lakes in Glarus, then tackles the Rüchigrat ridge to reach the broad upper plateau and summit. The hike is serious T3 mountain terrain — sustained, with some steep sections and route-finding — but entirely non-technical and free of fixed ropes. The solitude is striking: this corner of Glarus sees a fraction of the crowds found in the Jungfrau region.

Routes (standard route, SAC T3)

1. Braunwald → Oberblegisee → Rüchigrat → Bächistock — standard ascent T3

  • Start at Braunwald (1256 m) — reached by Braunwaldbahn funicular from Linthal.
  • Follow the marked trail south-east through alpine meadows toward the Bleg alp area.
  • Reach the Oberblegisee (~1755 m) — a beautiful lake with Glärnisch reflections; excellent rest stop.
  • Ascend steeper terrain beyond the lake, gaining the Rüchigrat ridge via a series of switchbacks.
  • Follow the ridge line south toward the upper Glärnisch plateau — terrain becomes rockier and more exposed.
  • Final ascent to Bächistock (2914 m) — broad rocky summit with panoramic views of the Glarner Alps, Linth valley, and Uri Alps.
  • Return by the same route. Descent takes ~2.5–3 h.

2. Variant: loop via Rüti or Klöntal side T3+

  • Experienced hikers can descend to the Klöntal (Klöntalersee) on the north-west side, or loop via Rüti.
  • These variants add distance and require careful navigation — consult a detailed topo map.
  • Not recommended on first ascent or in poor visibility.

Getting There

By car

Drive to Linthal (Glarus, GL). Parking is available at the Braunwaldbahn valley station in Linthal. Note: Braunwald itself is car-free — you must leave your vehicle in Linthal and take the funicular.

From Zürich: A3 south to Ziegelbrücke, then cantonal road via Glarus to Linthal (~90 min).

By public transport (recommended)

  • Train: Zürich HB → Linthal — S-Bahn or IC to Ziegelbrücke, change to the Südostbahn (SOB) / SBB regional train via Glarus to Linthal. Journey ~1 h 20 min–1 h 40 min.
  • Braunwaldbahn funicular: Linthal station → Braunwald (~10 min, runs approx. every 30 min). Buy a combined SBB+funicular ticket or pay separately at the valley station.
  • Plan connections via sbb.ch.
  • Braunwald being car-free is a feature, not a bug — the funicular ride is part of the experience.

Summer hike only. Best window: late June to mid-October. Snow typically clears from the Rüchigrat by late June; the upper plateau may hold late-season snow through July in heavy snow years. Check current conditions with SLF and the SAC situation reports.

Trip Reports (hikr.org)

Key takeaways

  • The Oberblegisee is a genuine highlight — arrive by 10:00–10:30 for the best reflections and morning light on the Glärnisch face. Many hikers stop here and turn back; the ascent above the lake is where the real mountain terrain begins.
  • The Rüchigrat approach involves sustained steep terrain on mixed grass and rock — trekking poles are highly recommended, especially for the descent. The path is marked but narrow in places.
  • The upper plateau is remarkably flat and open for a summit of this scale. Views extend to the Uri Alps, Tödi, the Linth valley, and on clear days toward the Bernese Oberland. There is no summit hut — bring everything you need.
  • Braunwald's car-free character is part of the appeal: the funicular ride, the village atmosphere, and the absence of traffic make the start and end of the day unusually relaxed. The Braunwaldbahn runs until early evening — check the last departure before setting out.

Gear Checklist

Mandatory

  • Sturdy hiking boots (B-grade, ankle support) — the Rüchigrat has loose rock
  • 2–3 L water — no reliable water sources above Oberblegisee
  • Food + energy snacks for 6–7 h
  • Sun protection: hat, sunglasses, SPF 30+
  • Warm layers: insulating mid-layer + windproof/waterproof shell
  • Gloves + warm hat — summit can be near freezing even in summer
  • Headlamp (in case of slower-than-expected descent)
  • Phone with offline SwissTopo map + GPX downloaded
  • Small first-aid kit
  • Valid SBB ticket + cash for funicular

Recommended

  • Trekking poles — highly recommended for descent on steep terrain
  • Power bank (no charging at the summit)
  • Whistle
  • Binoculars (Uri Alps panorama is spectacular)

Good to know

  • Braunwald has a small grocery and several restaurants — stock up / refuel here
  • No hut on this route; the Oberblegisee is the natural lunch/break spot on ascent

Safety & Hazards

  • Sustained T3 terrain: the Rüchigrat and upper plateau involve steep grass, scree, and rocky sections. A slip can be serious — take it slow and test holds.
  • Thunderstorms: the upper ridge is fully exposed with no shelter. Descend immediately if clouds build rapidly. Start early (before 06:30 from Zürich) to reach the summit before midday.
  • Snow: late snow can persist on the north-facing approaches into July. Check conditions with SLF and recent trip reports before setting out early in the season.
  • Route-finding: path markings thin out on the upper plateau in poor visibility. Download the GPX track and have SwissTopo offline.
  • Retreating glacier: the Glärnisch ice is shrinking — terrain near the former glacier margin can be unstable and slippery. Stay on the marked route.
  • Last funicular: check the Braunwaldbahn evening timetable before you start — missing the last car means a steep descent to Linthal on foot (+1 h, rough trail).
  • Emergency: Rega 1414 or 112. Mobile reception is patchy on the upper plateau — download maps offline.
