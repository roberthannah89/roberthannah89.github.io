Routes

Mülenen → Niesen Kulm — the classic "Swiss Pyramid" ascent — A relentless but non-technical 1700 m climb from the valley floor, gaining elevation through pasture, forest, and alpine terrain. Iconic views of Lake Thun dominate the descent. T2

  • Reichenbach trailhead (707m) — Start point; zigzag forest trail
  • Stöpfflue (1105m) — First elevation pass; meet asphalt road
  • Schwandegg Station (1666m) — Intermediate funicular station; enters more open terrain
  • Glogghore (1984m) — Ridge waypoint with serpentine paths
  • Räbmättli (~2150m) — High meadow; final approach begins
  • Niesen Kulm summit (2362m) — Restaurant and panoramic views of Lake Thun

Getting There

By car

Via Simmental: From Thun, drive south toward Spiez, then follow the scenic Simmental valley road toward Mülenen (1680m elevation, ~40 km from Thun center, ~45–50 min). Excellent parking at the Mülenen trailhead near the valley station; ample space and facilities. Alternatively, approach from Bern via Spiez. Road access is open year-round but can be busy on weekends.

By public transport

Train + PostBus: From Thun (main station), take the regional train toward Spiez (~10 min), then board PostBus line 1 or 2 toward Mülenen (~30 min from Spiez, final stop). Combined SBB + PostBus journey ≈60 min total. From Interlaken, take the scenic train to Thun (~20 min) then proceed as above. Swiss Travel Pass covers both; check current PostBus route numbers on sbb.ch.

Lapse-rate temperature estimate

Mountain temperature drops about 6.5 °C per 1000 m.

  • Valley reference: Thun (428 m) — main valley reference for Thunersee region.
  • Summit: 2362 m → ~1762 m above the reference → subtract ~10.6 °C.
  • 20 °C at Thun → ~7 °C at Niesen Kulm (2362 m) — typical July afternoon. Wind exposure on the summit is high; bring extra layers.
  • Pack for the cold end: hat, gloves, windproof, insulating layer.

Snow / season

July–September is optimal: stable weather, minimal snow, warm(ish) days. June can see snow patches above 1800 m. October deteriorates with increasing cloud and wind. The Niesen-Bahn funicular operates May–October, so plan descent accordingly if using the railway.

Trip Reports (hikr.org)

Key takeaways

  • Relentless steep gain from valley floor: Unbroken 1700m ascent; no real warm-up section. Valley base (707m) is unusually low; switchbacks don't relieve grade until mid-slope.
  • Overwhelming funicular tourist crowds: Masses of non-hiking tourists using the train create heavy congestion on upper trails. Serious hikers avoid Schwandegg section during peak train times (1:30–2:30pm).
  • 'Bähnli-Berg' paradox: Funicular makes it a tourist destination; lower section from Reichenbach offers escape from crowds but adds 1700m vs. using mid-station. Upper trail becomes very crowded 10:00–16:00.

Safety & Hazards

  • Unrelenting grade: Steep ascent throughout; no terrain flattening until upper meadows. Recovery time limited; cumulative fatigue risk.
  • Weather exposure: Pyramid shape + 2362m elevation = exposed ridge; afternoon thunderstorms common May–September.
  • Trail congestion: Summer crowds peak 10:00–16:00; tight single-track on upper sections; traffic jams during turnaround.
  • Descent risk: Long downhill pounding on knees; loose scree on alternative descents.
  • Spring/winter transitions: Snowmelt creates water channels; icy sections on north-facing slopes (April, Oct–Nov).
  • Heat in July–Aug: South-facing slopes offer minimal shade; dehydration risk on steep sections.
  • Turn back if thunderstorms develop — lightning risk above treeline is extreme.
