Chäserrugg 2262 m

Ridge walk via Iltios cable car — SAC T3 · ~1000 m gain · ~12 km · ~5–6 h

"Chäserrugg is the highest of the seven flat-topped Churfirsten peaks, their distinctive table-top silhouette visible from across north-eastern Switzerland. The ridge walk above the treeline offers one of the most spectacular panoramas in the Swiss Alps: Walensee shimmering blue in the valley, Bodensee glittering on the northern horizon, and the Appenzell and Vorarlberg Alps rolling east."
— Route notes

The Churfirsten (7 peaks east to west: Chäserrugg, Hinterrugg, Zuestoll, Brisi, Selun, Frümsel, Gauschla) form a near-horizontal limestone wall above Walensee. Chäserrugg (2262 m) is the easternmost and highest, linked to neighbouring Hinterrugg (2306 m) by a narrow ridge. The standard route uses the Unterwasser–Iltios cable car (1317 m) to bypass 400 m of forest, then follows the open Churfirsten crest with moderate exposure to the summit. A Herzog & de Meuron–designed Bergrestaurant sits at the top — coffee with a sheer-drop view included.

Routes (from the SAC Route Portal and local guides)

1. Via Iltios cable car → Selun → Chäserrugg — standard route T3

  • Start: Unterwasser, Toggenburg (~910 m). Take the cable car to Iltios (1317 m) to save ~400 m of forest ascent.
  • From Iltios, follow the broad ridge path north-east, gaining altitude steadily on open alpine terrain above the treeline.
  • Pass via Selun (~2205 m), one of the Churfirsten ridge peaks, with views opening toward Walensee.
  • Final push to Chäserrugg summit (2262 m): some exposed ledges and rocky sections (T3).
  • Return the same way, or continue to Hinterrugg (2306 m) for a ridge extension.
  • Total from Iltios: ~12 km round-trip, ~700 m gain, ~4–5 h. From Unterwasser: ~1000 m gain, ~5–6 h.

2. Chäserrugg + Hinterrugg ridge extension T3+

  • From Chäserrugg, the narrow ridge to Hinterrugg (2306 m) is the highest point of the Churfirsten.
  • Exposed traverse with good holds — stay on the marked path. Adds ~1–2 h and requires sure-footedness.
  • Descent back via Iltios cable car — confirm last departure time before committing.

3. Full Churfirsten traverse (Chäserrugg → Gauschla) T4

  • The classic multi-peak traverse covers all seven Churfirsten summits to the Gauschla (2075 m) in the west.
  • Very long day (10+ h); T4 experience and route-finding ability required on unmarked sections.
  • Descent to Flums or Unterterzen (Walensee) on the north side, or return transport from Walenstadt.
  • Not recommended as a first Churfirsten outing — do the T3 standard route first.

Difficulty

This hike is graded SAC T3. Demanding mountain hiking with possible exposure and a real need for sure-footedness.

Getting There

By car

Drive to Unterwasser (Toggenburg, SG). Parking at or near the cable car base station. The valley road runs from Wattwil (A1 junction) or from Bad Ragaz via Flums and the Walensee north shore.

Public transport from Zürich (~2 h)

  • Train: Zürich HB → Wattwil (~55 min, S-Bahn S7 or IR 15 toward Chur).
  • Postbus 740: Wattwil → Unterwasser (~35 min, several per hour in summer).
  • Total door-to-door from Zürich: ~2 h. Buy cable car ticket on site at Unterwasser.
  • Plan via sbb.ch.

Trip Reports (hikr.org)

Key takeaways

  • The cable car from Unterwasser to Iltios is strongly recommended — it saves 400 m of steep forest and opens 4+ hours of ridge hiking. Verify opening dates on churfirsten.ch before going.
  • The ridge path from Iltios to Chäserrugg is well-marked but includes several exposed ledge sections with a significant drop to the north (Walensee, ~1800 m below). Vertigo-prone hikers should assess the T3 rating carefully.
  • Summit panorama is exceptional on clear days: Walensee and Walenstadt directly below to the north, Bodensee on the northern horizon, Säntis and Appenzell Alps to the east, Graubünden Alps (Ringelspitz, Piz Sardona) to the south-east.
  • The Bergrestaurant Chäserrugg (Herzog & de Meuron design, 2016) is a unique architectural highlight — excellent coffee and a terrace with sheer-drop views over Walensee. Reserve a table for lunch in high season.
  • Cattle graze on the Iltios alp in mid-summer — give mother cows with calves wide berth.

Gear Checklist

Mandatory

  • Sturdy hiking boots (B-grade, ankle support) — limestone ridge slippery when wet
  • 2–3 L water (no reliable sources on the upper ridge)
  • Food + emergency snack
  • Sun: hat, sunglasses, SPF 30+ (exposed ridge, high UV)
  • Layers: base + insulating + windproof / rainproof shell (ridge wind)
  • Hat + gloves (summit often 5–10 °C cooler than valley with wind chill)
  • Headlamp
  • Phone with offline SwissTopo map + GPX
  • Small first-aid kit
  • Cash / card for cable car and Bergrestaurant

Recommended

  • Trekking poles (helpful on the steep descent from Selun)
  • Power bank
  • Binoculars (Walensee + Bodensee panorama)
  • Whistle

Hinterrugg / full traverse only

  • Helmet (for full traverse on unmarked ridge sections)
  • Significantly more time and an early start
  • T4 route-finding experience; compass/GPS backup

Safety & Hazards

  • Exposure: the Chäserrugg ridge has a steep northern drop (~1800 m to Walensee); several ledge sections on the T3 route. A slip can be very serious.
  • Limestone: excellent grip when dry — extremely slippery when wet. Do not attempt in or just after rain.
  • Thunderstorms: the Churfirsten crest offers zero shelter. Descend immediately if storms develop — they form quickly in Toggenburg, often by early afternoon.
  • Cable car last departure: missing it means a long, steep forest descent. Check the schedule at the valley station and build in buffer time.
  • Cattle: mother cows with calves graze on the Iltios alp in summer — give them space.
  • Phone reception: generally good on the summit; can be patchy on descent paths. Download SwissTopo maps offline.
  • Emergency: Rega 1414 or European emergency 112.
  • This is a summer / autumn route. Winter and spring involve avalanche terrain and snow-covered limestone — very different gear and experience required.
