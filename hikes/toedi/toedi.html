Tödi 3614 m

Linthal → Fridolinshütte — SAC T4 · ~1460 m gain · ~8 km one-way · overnight option

"Tödi is the highest point in the canton of Glarus — a massive glacier mountain that guards the head of the Linth valley. The approach to the Fridolinshütte is one of the great valley walks of the Glarus Alps: remote, dramatic, and increasingly wild as the glacier comes into view."

This hike goes to the Fridolinshütte SAC hut (2111 m), the classic base camp for summit attempts on Tödi. The route from Linthal through the Limmerental valley is graded T4 — sure-footedness and alpine experience are required, but no glacier equipment. The final push to the summit (3614 m) requires crampons, ice axe, and rope; that section is rated T5+/PD and is not part of this hike. The hut itself — with its stunning glacier panorama — is a magnificent destination in its own right, either as a long day hike or an overnight before a summit bid.

Routes (Linthal → Fridolinshütte via Limmerental)

1. Linthal → Fridolinshütte — standard T4 approach T4

  • Start at Linthal train station (~650 m). Follow the valley road / trail into the Limmerental.
  • The path ascends steadily through the Limmerental — a long, progressively remote alpine valley.
  • Pass Tierfed (~1100 m), where the terrain becomes more demanding and trail conditions more variable.
  • Upper section involves rocky path, route-finding on alpine terrain, and some exposed passages — characteristic T4 ground.
  • Arrive at Fridolinshütte SAC hut (2111 m) — spectacular glacier panorama of Tödi and Bifertenstock.
  • Ascent: ~1460 m gain, ~8 km, 4–5 h. Descent: ~3 h. Total round-trip ~7–8 h.
  • Overnight option: book the hut, rest, then ascend to the summit (T5+/PD, glacier equipment required) the next morning.

2. Summit ascent: Fridolinshütte → Tödi summit (3614 m) — T5+/PD T5+/PD

  • NOT covered by this hike page. The summit requires alpine mountaineering skills.
  • From the hut, cross the glacier (crampons + ice axe + rope mandatory). Crevasse hazard — guided or very experienced parties only.
  • Typically undertaken as an early-morning start from the hut (alpine start ~3–5 am).
  • This extension is rated PD in the UIAA/SAC alpine scale — not a hiking grade.

Getting There

By car

Drive to Linthal via the A3 motorway → Glarus → cantonal road through the Linth valley. Parking available at Linthal train station. The road beyond Linthal into the Limmerental may have seasonal restrictions — confirm locally. Some visitors drive further to Tierfed (limited parking) to shorten the approach by ~4 km each way.

By public transport

  • Train: Zürich HB → Linthal (~1.5 h). Typical route: Zürich HB → Ziegelbrücke → Schwanden → Linthal (change at Ziegelbrücke or Schwanden; check sbb.ch).
  • Alternatively: Zürich HB → Glarus → Linthal (S-Bahn to Glarus then Glarner regional train).
  • Linthal is the terminus of the Glarner regional railway — frequent trains from Schwanden and Glarus.
  • Return trains from Linthal run into the evening — check SBB for last departure.

Snow / season

Summer hike only. Fridolinshütte is staffed June–September. Outside this window the hut is unstaffed (emergency shelter only), and significant snow may cover the upper trail. Do not attempt without hut confirmation and current snow conditions.

Trip Reports (hikr.org)

Key takeaways

  • The first part of the route from Linthal through the lower Limmerental is straightforward valley hiking — but the trail becomes increasingly demanding as you approach the upper cirque and the Fridolinshütte. Budget 4–5 h for the ascent and do not underestimate the upper T4 section.
  • The Fridolinshütte is a genuine alpine hut, not a restaurant — book well in advance (especially July/August weekends) and check staffing dates on the SAC website. Arriving without a booking on a busy summer weekend is a gamble.
  • Glacier approach from the hut (for those attempting the summit) requires a very early alpine start (~3–4 am), a rope team, crampons, and ice axe. Crevasse danger on the Bifertenfirn glacier is real — unroped travel is not appropriate.
  • The Limmerental valley offers one of the most dramatic enclosed glacier panoramas in the northern Swiss Alps — the hike to the hut is fully rewarding even without any summit ambition.

Safety & Hazards

  • T4 terrain: the upper section of the Limmerental involves rocky, loose, and potentially wet terrain. A slip can be serious — focus and sure-footedness required.
  • Glacier hazard above the hut: the Tödi glacier (Bifertenfirn) is immediately above the hut. Do not venture onto the glacier without crampons, ice axe, rope, and glacier travel experience — crevasses are present.
  • Afternoon thunderstorms: the Glarus Alps see frequent convective storms in summer. Be at the hut or well into descent by 13:00–14:00 if instability is forecast.
  • Snow: the upper trail may carry significant snow from late spring through June, and from October. Check current conditions with the hut guardian before setting out.
  • Hut reservation: the Fridolinshütte fills quickly on summer weekends. Do not arrive without a booking. The hut guardian can be reached via the SAC hut portal.
  • Remote terrain: the upper Limmerental has limited phone reception. Download offline maps (SwissTopo / Swisstopo app) and carry the GPX track.
  • Emergency: Rega helicopter rescue 1414 · European emergency 112.
  • This is a summer-only route. Winter ascent involves serious avalanche terrain — the valley is avalanche-prone in winter and spring.
