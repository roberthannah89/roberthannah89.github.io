Switzerland's most photographed easy alpine hike: traverse past five glacier-fed lakes—Riffelsee, Stellisee, Grindjesee, and more—with the Matterhorn reflected in crystalline waters at every turn. Accessible via the Gornergrat Railway from car-free Zermatt, this quintessential Valais experience combines accessibility with breathtaking alpine scenery.

Getting There

By car

From Basel / Zurich / Geneva: Drive via A2/A9 motorway towards Valais. Exit at Visp or Brig, then head south to Zermatt (63 km from Visp, approximately 1.5 hours). Parking in Zermatt: No personal cars allowed in Zermatt village. Leave your car at a parking facility: Parkhaus Zermatt: Large multi-story car park near the train station entrance (CHF 15–20/day). Parking Winkelmatten: Larger lot on the outskirts, slightly cheaper. Free parking: Some spaces available along Bahnhofstrasse early morning (limited, first-come basis). Free shuttle bus runs frequently between car parks and village centre. Walk to Gornergrat railway station (5–10 min) or use electric taxis (Zermatt is car-free).

By public transport

By Train: Take the main rail line to Visp or Brig (via Zurich, Bern, or Geneva). Visp to Zermatt: Take the Matterhorn Gotthard Bahn (MGB) regional train from Visp to Zermatt (1.5 hours, runs hourly). Zermatt to Gornergrat: From Zermatt train station, walk 50 m to the Gornergrat Bahn (GGB) mountain railway station. The rack railway climbs 1,620 m vertically in just 9 km, reaching Gornergrat in approximately 40 minutes. Departures every 20–30 minutes in summer (typically 07:00–16:30, with later departures on clear evenings). Journey time from Geneva: Approximately 4–5 hours total (train + transfers). Swiss Travel Pass / Half-Fare Card: Valid on all mountain railways in the region.

Lapse-rate temperature estimate

Mountain temperature drops about 6.5 °C per 1000 m.

  • Valley reference: Zermatt (1,620 m).
  • Summit: 3135 m → ~2535 m above the reference → subtract ~15.2 °C.
  • Zermatt +20°C → Gornergrat approximately +5°C (roughly 1°C per 300 m elevation gain). Example: If 20°C in Zermatt, expect 5–8°C on Gornergrat with significant wind chill.
  • Pack for the cold end: hat, gloves, windproof, insulating layer.

Snow / season

Best season: June–September. July–August offers the most stable weather and shortest daylight hours are still sufficient for full-day tours. June: Snow possible above 3,000 m, glacier crossings may be risky. July–August: Optimal conditions; high tourist traffic. September: Fewer crowds, reliable weather, still excellent conditions. October onwards: Significant snow, railway may close during storms. Weather hazards: Rapid afternoon thunderstorms (common June–August). Strong föhn winds from south. UV intensity extreme at altitude—bring high-SPF sunscreen. Temperature can drop rapidly; layer clothing. Wind-chill makes conditions feel 10–15°C colder than actual temperature.
