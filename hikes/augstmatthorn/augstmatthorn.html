Augstmatthorn 2137 m

Habkern → Lombachalp → Augstmatthorn — SAC T3 · ~1200 m gain · ~14 km loop · ~6 h

"Der Brienzergrat ist einer der schmalsten und ausgesetztesten Grasgrate der Alpen — eine Messerschneide zwischen Brienzersee und Habkerntal mit dramatischen Tiefblicken auf beiden Seiten."
— hikr.org (paraphrased)

The Augstmatthorn (2137 m) is the eastern signature peak of the Brienzergrat, a narrow grass-and-limestone ridge running ~20 km along the north shore of Lake Brienz. Often called a "mini Hardergrat": same knife-edge grass character as its longer neighbour to the west, but with bailouts that make it accessible as a single-day T3 from Habkern. Famous for resident ibex (Steinbock) on the south flank and dramatic 1500 m drops to the lake.

Routes (Brienzergrat — north-side ascent from Habkern)

1. Habkern → Lombachalp → Augstmatthorn (loop) — standard route T3

  • Start: Habkern (1056 m), reached by PostBus 104 from Interlaken West.
  • Up the Lombach valley to Lombachalp (~1525 m) — broad pastoral basin, ~2 h of T1/T2 farm tracks and trails.
  • From Lombachalp climb the north flank to the ridge between Suggiture and Augstmatthorn (~2050 m), then follow the Brienzergrat east to the summit cross at 2137 m.
  • Ridge character: narrow grass crest with steep drops south to Lake Brienz; T3 sure-footedness essential, no scrambling required when dry.
  • Descent: reverse the route via Lombachalp, or loop via the Suggiture/Hohgant trail back into the Lombach valley.
  • Total: ~14 km, ~1200 m gain, ~6 h moving.

2. Brienzergrat traverse — Augstmatthorn → Niederhorn (point-to-point) T3

  • Continue west from the summit along the full Brienzergrat: Augstmatthorn → Suggiture (2085 m) → Gemmenalphorn (2061 m) → Niederhorn (1963 m).
  • ~10 km of continuous narrow grass ridge, mostly T3 with short T3+ steps; the easiest end of the Hardergrat family.
  • Bailout: from any saddle, descend north into the Habkern/Lombach basin (well-marked) or south to the Beatenberg cable-car stations.
  • Finish: ride the Niederhorn cable car down to Beatenberg → funicular to Interlaken.
  • Adds ~3–4 h to the day; viable as a one-way variant in good weather, not in fog.

3. Full Hardergrat traverse — Harder Kulm → Augstmatthorn (extreme) T5

  • The famous Hardergrat: ~22 km of unbroken knife-edge grass from Interlaken to the Augstmatthorn.
  • Sustained T5 exposure on grass — slip-and-die terrain. Not for beginners under any circumstances.
  • Out of scope for this plan; mentioned as context only.

Getting There

By car

Drive to Habkern (Bern) via Interlaken — ~15 km up the Habkerntal road from Interlaken Ost. Free / paid parking at the village or at the upper Lombachalp parking (saves ~1.5 h on foot).

By public transport (recommended)

  • Train: Zürich HB → Bern → Interlaken West (≈2 h end-to-end).
  • PostBus 104 Interlaken West → Habkern, Post (~25 min).
  • Total Zürich → Habkern: ~2.5 h door-to-door.
  • If finishing on the Niederhorn: cable car Niederhorn → Beatenberg Station → funicular to Interlaken Ost → train home.
  • Plan via sbb.ch.

Snow / season

Summer hike only. Best window: late June to mid-October, when the grass is dry and the snow is fully off the north flank. Spring snow lingers on the north side until mid-June.

Trip Reports (hikr.org)

Key takeaways

  • Most reports start from Habkern or the Lombachalp upper parking and reach the summit in 3–4 h.
  • The final ridge from Suggiture to the Augstmatthorn cross is the highlight — ~30–45 min of narrow grass with steep south drops to Lake Brienz.
  • Ibex (Steinbock) sightings on the south flank are very common in the early morning; bring a zoom lens.
  • Continuing west to Niederhorn doubles the ridge time and is highly recommended in stable weather; bailouts exist at every saddle.
  • Universal warning: do not attempt in or after rain — the grass becomes unsafe.

Safety & Hazards

  • WET GRASS IS THE #1 HAZARD. The Brienzergrat is steep grass on both sides; once it is wet (rain, dew, fog) the T3 grade is no longer realistic and a slip on the south side has fatal consequences. If it has rained in the last 12 hours, choose a different objective.
  • Exposure: the summit ridge is a knife-edge with ~1500 m of drop south to Lake Brienz. Sure-footedness essential; not for those uncomfortable with heights.
  • Not for beginners. T3 demands experience with exposed mountain hiking. If unsure, hire a guide or pick a T2.
  • Thunderstorms: the ridge is fully exposed for the entire summit section. Descend early if convection is forecast.
  • Ibex: protected wildlife — observe quietly from the trail, do not approach or feed.
  • Cattle: mother cows with calves on Lombachalp can be defensive; give wide berth.
  • Phone reception: intermittent on the ridge. Download maps offline.
  • Emergency: Rega 1414 or 112.
  • This is a summer route. Out of season the north flank holds snow and the grass is unstable.
