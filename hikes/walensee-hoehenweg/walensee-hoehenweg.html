Walensee Höhenweg (Leistcamm) 1350 m

Walenstadt → Betlis → Quinten → Weesen — SAC T2 · ~800 m gain · ~18 km one-way · ~6–7 h

The Walensee Höhenweg (Switzerland Mobility route 63) is one of Switzerland's most dramatic cliff traverses: a ~18 km one-way crossing of the near-vertical limestone south shore of Lake Walen (Walensee), connecting Walenstadt in the east to Weesen in the west. For much of the route the path clings to steep cliff faces high above the turquoise lake, with the Churfirsten ridge towering across the water to the north. Fixed ropes assist on the most exposed ledges; the route is rated SAC T2 but demands a firm head for heights.

Midway, the path drops to lake level at Quinten — a car-free hamlet famous for its improbably Mediterranean microclimate: fig trees, palm trees, and a lakeside restaurant reachable only by boat or on foot. After Quinten the route climbs back onto the cliffs before the final descent into Weesen. Return to Walenstadt is by Walensee Schifffahrt boat or a short train ride.

Routes (Switzerland Mobility route 63)

1. Walenstadt → Weesen — full traverse (standard direction) T2

  • Start at Walenstadt train station (425 m). Follow signs for SM route 63 south then west along the lake shore.
  • The path climbs steeply onto the south-shore cliffs via forest and rock ledges to reach the Leistcamm high section (~1350 m). Fixed ropes assist the most vertiginous passages.
  • Betlis (~430 m, ~6 km) — small settlement directly below the cliffs; path descends to and re-ascends from lake level.
  • Quinten (~430 m, ~11 km) — the route's highlight: lakeside restaurant, Mediterranean trees, car-free village. Allow 30–60 min here.
  • West of Quinten the path climbs back onto the cliffs for the final exposed section before a long descent to Weesen (420 m, ~18 km).
  • Return to Walenstadt: Walensee Schifffahrt boat (summer season, ~1 h scenic crossing) or train via Ziegelbrücke/Sargans.

2. Weesen → Walenstadt — reverse direction T2

  • Identical route in reverse; some sections feel steeper on ascent from the Weesen side.
  • Advantage: arrive at Quinten before the midday crowds and enjoy the lake view 'looking east' toward the Churfirsten.
  • Train from Walenstadt back to Zürich is straightforward; no boat dependence.

3. Walenstadt → Quinten (half-day option) T2

  • Cover only the eastern half of the route (~11 km to Quinten).
  • Return to Walenstadt or Murg by Walensee Schifffahrt boat from Quinten landing stage.
  • Ideal for a shorter outing or when afternoon thunderstorms are forecast.

Getting There

By car

Driving is possible but complicates return logistics on this linear route. Options:

  • Park in Walenstadt (free parking near station), hike to Weesen, return by boat or train.
  • Leave a second car in Weesen (or drop-off) and park the other in Walenstadt.
  • Walenstadt is reached from Zürich via A3 motorway (Sargans exit), ~60 km.

Public transport (recommended — linear route!)

  • Train: Zürich HB → Walenstadt (direct IC/IR, ≈45 min). Check sbb.ch.
  • Start hiking from Walenstadt station; trailhead signs are visible from the platform exit.
  • Return from Weesen:
    • Boat (recommended): Walensee Schifffahrt — scenic ~1 h crossing, runs late April–October. Check schedule online.
    • Train: Weesen → Ziegelbrücke → Sargans → Walenstadt (≈40 min + changes).
  • Alternatively: train/boat to Walenstadt and start in reverse from Weesen.

Lapse-rate temperature estimate

Mountain temperature drops about 6.5 °C per 1000 m.

  • Valley reference: Walenstadt, ~425 m.
  • Summit: 1350 m → ~925 m above the reference → subtract ~6 °C.
  • Example: Walenstadt forecast 15–24 °C → trail high point ≈ 9–18 °C. Wind chill on the exposed clifftops can be significant.
  • Pack for the cold end: hat, gloves, windproof, insulating layer.

Sources to check

  • MeteoSwiss — use the Walenstadt or Weesen point forecast.
  • Meteoblue Walenstadt — hourly forecast and wind.
  • Meteoblue Weesen — western end of the lake.
  • Thunderstorms are the main risk — the cliffs have no shelter and lightning exposure is high. Turn back or sit out in Quinten if storms build.

Snow / season

Best season: April – October. This is a low-altitude route (max ~1350 m) so it is often snow-free from April. The south-facing cliffs also dry very quickly after rain. Quinten's microclimate is reliably warm and sheltered. Avoid icy or wet rock on the cliff sections — conditions degrade quickly in autumn rain.

Trip Reports (hikr.org)

Key takeaways

  • The route undulates considerably — cumulative gain is ~800 m even though the lake sits at 419 m and the high point is only ~1350 m. Expect repeated ascents and descents, not a sustained ridge walk.
  • Bring enough water for the full traverse: sources are scarce on the clifftops between Walenstadt and Betlis. Fill up at Quinten's fountain if needed.
  • The cliff path between Betlis and Quinten is the crux — narrow ledges with fixed ropes and significant drop below. Hands-free balance recommended; trekking poles may need to be stowed.
  • Quinten's Gasthof Rössli is the only restaurant on the route — plan your lunch timing accordingly (it can fill up in summer). The ferry from Quinten is a popular bail-out.
  • The Walensee ferry (Walensee Schifffahrt) running Weesen → Walenstadt is the most atmospheric return. Book or check times the night before — afternoon departures are infrequent.
  • T2 on good ground but not suitable for those with severe vertigo — the clifftop sections are genuinely exposed with drops straight into the lake.

Gear Checklist

Mandatory

  • Sturdy hiking boots (ankle support — path has loose rock and narrow ledges)
  • 2–3 L water (very limited sources on the cliffs; fill at Quinten if passing through early)
  • Food + emergency snack
  • Sun protection: hat, sunglasses, SPF 30+ (south-facing cliffs catch all-day sun)
  • Layers: windproof shell (exposed clifftops can be cold despite low elevation)
  • Headlamp (if there's any chance of finishing after dark)
  • Phone with offline SwissTopo or Swisstopo app + GPX track
  • Small first-aid kit

Recommended

  • Trekking poles (collapsible — may need stowing on the narrowest ledge sections)
  • Power bank
  • Cash for the Quinten restaurant and ferry ticket
  • Swimwear — Quinten has a small beach and the lake is swimmable in summer

Leave behind

  • Heavy pack — this is a long traverse; every kilogram matters on 18 km with 800 m gain
  • Dogs (not recommended on the exposed ledge sections — steep drops)

Safety & Hazards

  • Exposure: The clifftop sections above Betlis and Quinten are genuinely vertiginous — a slip above the lake is serious. Fixed ropes are in place but use them confidently.
  • Wet rock: Limestone is exceptionally slippery when wet. The south-facing walls dry quickly in sun but shaded ledges can remain damp for hours after rain.
  • Thunderstorms: The cliffs offer zero shelter. Check the MeteoSwiss convective forecast before departure; turn back or shelter at Quinten if storms develop.
  • Bail-out: Quinten (midpoint) is the only realistic escape via the Walensee ferry. There is no road access and no other exit between Walenstadt and Weesen.
  • Vertigo: T2 rating assumes confident movement on narrow paths with exposure. If you have doubts, do the Walenstadt → Quinten half-day only.
  • Phone reception: Patchy on the cliffs between Betlis and Quinten. Download maps offline before you go.
  • Ferry reliance: If returning by boat from Weesen or Quinten, check the last sailing time — missing it means a train detour.
  • Emergency: Rega 1414 or 112. GPS location essential when calling from the cliffside.
