Säntis 2502 m

Single-day Lisengrat traverse — Ebenalp cable car → Schäfler → Rotsteinpass → ridge → Säntis → Schwägalp on foot. SAC T4 · ~1950 m gain · ~16.4 km point-to-point · ~7–8 h

"The Lisengrat is the iconic ridge between Rotsteinpass and the Säntis summit — a continuously cabled limestone crest with breathtaking drops on both sides. Wet rock turns it lethal."
— Alpstein guidebook (paraphrased)

This page frames the Lisengrat ridge itself as a one-day objective, accessed via the Ebenalp cable car for an early start at 1640 m. You skip the ~770 m Wasserauen→Schäfler climb, traverse the Schäfler ridgeline, drop to Mesmer, climb to Rotsteinpass, cross the Lisengrat, summit Säntis, and descend on foot to Schwägalp — all in a single, weather-sensitive day.

Different from the 2-day plan. If you'd rather sleep at Berggasthaus Meglisalp and split the day, see the Wasserauen → Lisengrat → Schwägalp 2-day traverse. This page is the cable-car-assisted day-trip variant — same Lisengrat, longer total distance, no overnight.

2026 note: the Säntis Schwebebahn is closed for renewal all year. There is no cable-car descent option. You must walk down — to Schwägalp via Tierwies/Wagenlücke (point-to-point), or back over the Lisengrat to Meglisalp/Seealpsee/Wasserauen (loop). Plan accordingly.

Routes (both options assume the Säntis Schwebebahn is closed — descent on foot)

1. Ebenalp → Schäfler → Mesmer → Rotsteinpass → Lisengrat → Säntis → Schwägalp (point-to-point, planned) T4

  • Approach: Train to Wasserauen (868 m) → Ebenalp cable car (1640 m, ~6 min). On the trail by ~08:00 if you catch the first car.
  • Schäfler ridge (T2/T3): Ebenalp → Schäfler (1925 m, ~1.5 h). Open ridge with the Alpstein laid out to the south. Berggasthaus Schäfler is a coffee/water stop.
  • Mesmer dip (T2): Schäfler → Mesmer (~1600 m, ~1 h, ~325 m descent). Drops into the Mesmerloch alp basin — annoying re-loss of altitude but unavoidable on this line.
  • Rotsteinpass climb (T3): Mesmer → Rotsteinpass (2120 m, ~1.5–2 h, ~520 m gain). Steeper, rockier, with cables in the upper section. Berggasthaus Rotsteinpass at the col is a final water/refuge stop.
  • Lisengrat (T4 crux, ~50 min – 1.5 h): Rotsteinpass → Säntis along ~1.5–2 km of continuously cabled limestone ridge. Steel cables and pegs in exposed sections. 500 m+ drops on both sides. Quickly precarious when wet or snowy.
  • Säntis summit (2502 m): tourist hub with restaurant + weather station + cell coverage. Take the weather check seriously here — turning back on the Lisengrat is also an option.
  • Descent (T3, ~2.5–3 h): Säntis → Tierwies → Wagenlücke → Schwägalp (1352 m). Steep, rocky, well-trodden north flank. This is the only descent in 2026 — the cable car is closed.
  • Return: PostBus 791 from Schwägalp → Urnäsch → train Urnäsch → Gossau → Zürich (≈2.5 h).

2. Loop variant: Ebenalp → … → Säntis → back over Lisengrat → Meglisalp → Seealpsee → Wasserauen T4

  • Same approach + Lisengrat ascent as Route 1, but instead of dropping to Schwägalp, retrace the Lisengrat back to Rotsteinpass (~1 h, second exposure exposure) and descend the SE flank.
  • Rotsteinpass → Meglisalp (1517 m, ~1.5 h, T2) → Seealpsee (1141 m) → Wasserauen (868 m, ~1.5 h on lakeside path).
  • Total: ~9–10 h moving time — longer than Route 1, but no shuttle and the ridge is twice as good.
  • Pros: finishes back at Wasserauen (no PostBus juggling), classic Alpstein scenery on the descent, hut options at Meglisalp / Rotsteinpass if pace slips.
  • Cons: twice as much exposure on the Lisengrat — only do this if dry rock, calm wind, and confident on the cables.

3. Bail-out — turn back at Rotsteinpass T3

  • If the weather has turned by Rotsteinpass (forecast convection, gusty wind, wet rock), do not enter the Lisengrat.
  • Bail: Rotsteinpass → Meglisalp → Seealpsee → Wasserauen (~3–4 h, T2/T3). Solid Alpstein day in its own right.
  • Säntis stays for another time.

Getting There

By car

Drive to Wasserauen (Schwende-Rüte, AI). Pay parking at the train terminus, then take the Ebenalp cable car. Car-shuttle problem (Route 1): the route ends at Schwägalp, ~25 km away by road. Either leave a car at Schwägalp, or rely on the PostBus from Schwägalp via Urnäsch back toward Appenzell. Route 2 (loop) avoids this.

By public transport (recommended)

  • Outbound: Train Zürich HB → Gossau SG → Appenzell → Wasserauen (≈2 h end-to-end). Aim for an arrival by ~07:15 to catch the first Ebenalp cable car.
  • Ebenalp cable car: Wasserauen ↔ Ebenalp, ~6 min, ~CHF 36 single. ebenalp.ch. Season: typically May–Oct; first car ~07:30 (verify on the day).
  • Return Route 1 (Schwägalp): PostBus 791 Schwägalp → Urnäsch, train Urnäsch → Gossau → Zürich (≈2.5 h).
  • Return Route 2 (loop): straight back from Wasserauen on the same Appenzell-line trains.
  • Säntis Schwebebahn (Schwägalp ↔ Säntis): CLOSED ALL OF 2026 for full renewal. Plan on foot descent only — see saentisbahn.ch.
  • Plan via sbb.ch — Wasserauen and Schwägalp are both on the integrated SBB-PostBus ticket.

Lapse-rate temperature estimate

Mountain temperature drops about 6.5 °C per 1000 m.

  • Valley reference: Appenzell / Wasserauen, ~870 m.
  • Summit: 2502 m → ~1630 m above the reference → subtract ~11 °C.
  • Example: valley forecast 12–22 °C → summit ≈ +1 °C to +11 °C. Add wind chill on the ridge — gusts of 40 km/h on the Lisengrat are common.
  • Pack for the cold end: hat, gloves, windproof, insulating layer.

Sources to check

  • MeteoSwiss — most accurate Swiss forecast (use Schwende, Wasserauen, or Säntis summit station).
  • Meteoblue Säntis — forecast + nearby webcams.
  • Säntis hosts an official MeteoSwiss summit weather station — its live readings (temperature, wind, gust peaks) are public on the MeteoSwiss site. Read it the morning of.
  • Thunderstorms in the forecast → postpone or cancel. The Lisengrat is a fully exposed knife-edge ridge with steel cables. Lightning + wet rock = unsurvivable.
  • Wet rock alone is enough to turn back. The cabled holds become slippery; multiple trip reports flag the ridge as quickly precarious after rain or snow.

Snow / season

Summer hike only — July to early October. Snow can linger on the Lisengrat into early summer; the cabled sections become serious in any snow or ice. The Ebenalp cable car typically operates May–Oct; verify season + first-car time before going. Early starts beat afternoon thunderstorms — be off the ridge by 14:00.

Trip Reports (hikr.org)

Key takeaways

  • Lisengrat is rock-solid when dry — the cabled sections feel reasonable in T4 terms; it's the exposure (500 m+ drops on both sides), not the moves, that defines the grade.
  • Wet rock changes everything — multiple reports flag the ridge as "quickly precarious" after rain or snow. Cabled holds become slippery; bypass on a marginal weather day.
  • Schäfler → Mesmer → Rotsteinpass is longer than it looks — the ~325 m drop into Mesmer feels brutal mid-day. Plan ~4 h from Ebenalp to Rotsteinpass even moving well.
  • Crowds matter on weekends — Säntis is a tourist hub and the Lisengrat cables back up with traffic. Start early or pick a weekday.
  • Optimal window: July to early October for snow-free, dry conditions on the ridge.

Gear Checklist

Mandatory

  • Sturdy hiking boots (B/B+, ankle support — the Lisengrat is rocky and exposed)
  • 2.5–3 L water + electrolytes (top up at Schäfler / Rotsteinpass huts if open)
  • Food + emergency snack (long day; Säntis summit restaurant is a useful safety net)
  • Sun: hat, sunglasses, SPF 30+ — total exposure on the ridge
  • Layers: base + insulating + windproof / rainproof shell
  • Hat + light gloves (Säntis weather station has held Swiss low-temp records; ridge wind is real)
  • Headlamp (pre-dawn train start; useful insurance)
  • Phone with offline SwissTopo map + GPX
  • Small first-aid kit
  • Cash / card for the cable car, huts, and PostBus

Recommended for the Lisengrat

  • Helmet — light alpine helmet for rockfall on the ridge in busy conditions
  • Trekking poles (collapsible — strap to pack on the cabled sections)
  • Light gloves for grip on cold cables
  • A short via-ferrata set is overkill for T4 / grade A but reasonable if you're nervous on exposure

Optional

  • Power bank
  • Whistle
  • Lightweight bivvy / emergency blanket

Safety & Hazards

  • Lisengrat exposure: 500 m+ drops on both sides; cabled but committing. Sure-footedness and head for heights mandatory. Not for beginners.
  • Wet rock: the cabled sections become slippery fast. Postpone in or just after rain — no exceptions.
  • Thunderstorms: the ridge is a 1.5+ km fully exposed knife-edge. Lightning + steel cables + wet rock = unsurvivable. Watch the afternoon convection forecast and turn around at the first sign of buildup. Be off the ridge by 14:00.
  • Hard turn-around: if you're not at Rotsteinpass by 12:30, bail to Meglisalp. The Lisengrat is not a place to be late on.
  • 2026 cable-car closure: the Säntis Schwebebahn is closed all year. There is no descent option from the summit other than walking. If conditions deteriorate at the summit, the alternative is to retreat the way you came — which means a second Lisengrat crossing.
  • Snow / ice: shoulder-season patches on the Lisengrat and on the Tierwies/Wagenlücke descent can persist into July. If patches block the cabled sections, turn around — do not bypass on snow.
  • Cattle: Mesmer and Meglisalp pastures host mother cows with calves — give space, no eye contact.
  • Phone reception: good on the summit (cell tower) and Schäfler ridge, patchy in the Mesmer basin and on Lisengrat. Download maps offline.
  • Emergency: Rega 1414 or 112. The Säntis summit has staff, a restaurant, and the weather-station crew — useful safety net if conditions go bad.
  • This is a summer route. Winter ascent involves serious avalanche terrain on the north flank and ice on the Lisengrat — out of scope here.
