Kreuzberge 2123 m

Wasserauen → Seealpsee → Saxer Lücke → Kreuzberge — SAC T4 · ~1260 m gain · ~12 km · ~6–7 h round-trip

"The Kreuzberge traverse via Saxer Lücke is the eastern counterpart to the Lisengrat: a demanding, exposed limestone ridge route on the far side of the Alpstein, with views across the Rhine valley and back toward the Säntis massif."
— Alpstein ridge guidebook (paraphrased)

The Kreuzberge (also written Chrüzberg) are a chain of rocky limestone peaks on the eastern margin of the Alpstein, reaching ~2123 m. The standard approach from Wasserauen climbs first past the idyllic Seealpsee (1141 m), then steeply up to the dramatic notch of Saxer Lücke (1666 m) — one of the most atmospheric passes in the Appenzell Alps — before continuing along the cabled ridge to the Kreuzberge summits. Fixed ropes, exposed sections, and sure-footedness are required throughout. As the natural companion route to the Lisengrat, the Kreuzberge pairs perfectly for a 2-day Alpstein adventure.

Routes (from the SAC Route Portal and Alpstein guidebooks)

1. Wasserauen → Seealpsee → Saxer Lücke → Kreuzberge — standard T4 route T4

  • Wasserauen (856 m) → Seealpsee (1141 m): ~1–1.5 h, ~285 m gain. Well-signed hiking trail through forest; T2 to start. The Seealpsee is a classic Alpstein lake — popular but beautiful.
  • Seealpsee → Saxer Lücke (1666 m): ~1.5–2 h, ~525 m gain. Steep switchbacks up the valley wall; trail narrows and steepens. T3 on approach, becoming T4 near the pass itself.
  • Saxer Lücke (1666 m) → Kreuzberge (~2123 m): ~1.5–2 h, ~457 m gain. Exposed limestone ridge with fixed ropes in the steepest sections. Hands needed. Big drops on both sides. T4 throughout.
  • Return: same route in reverse, ~2.5–3 h. Allow extra time for steeper descent sections.
  • Note: Saxer Lücke and the ridge become quickly precarious when wet — the limestone is extremely slippery in damp conditions.

2. Saxer Lücke traverse (point-to-point, east side descent) T4

  • Continue from the Kreuzberge summit ridge down the eastern flank toward Frümsen (SG) or Sax for a point-to-point finish.
  • Requires shuttle logistics or a long return via postbus through Altstätten.
  • Eastern descent is also T4 and fully exposed — not a logical escape route in bad weather.

3. Saxer Lücke — Meglisalp combination (2-day Alpstein traverse) T3–T4

  • Day 1: Wasserauen → Seealpsee → Meglisalp (1517 m); overnight at Berggasthaus Meglisalp.
  • Day 2: Meglisalp → Saxer Lücke → Kreuzberge → return to Wasserauen.
  • This makes Kreuzberge the headline of a 2-day Alpstein adventure — pairs naturally with the Säntis/Lisengrat 2-day from the same valley.

Getting There

By car

Drive to Wasserauen (Schwende-Rüte, Appenzell Innerrhoden). Pay parking at the Appenzellerbahn terminus. Round-trip hike — same trailhead for start and finish.

By public transport

  • Train: Zürich HB → Gossau SG → Appenzell → Wasserauen (Appenzellerbahn, ≈2 h end-to-end). Hourly service in summer.
  • From St. Gallen: IC/IR to Gossau, then AB to Wasserauen (~50 min).
  • Direct connections also from Herisau and Gais (AR).
  • Plan via sbb.ch — Wasserauen is the Appenzellerbahn terminus.

Snow / season

Summer hike only. Best window: late June to mid-October. Snow can persist on the north-facing sections of the ridge into early summer. Limestone becomes dangerously slippery in any precipitation — do not attempt in or after rain.

Trip Reports (hikr.org)

Key takeaways

  • The Saxer Lücke section (1141–1666 m) is steep and tiring but well-marked; the real T4 begins immediately above the pass where the ridge narrows and drops appear on both sides.
  • Fixed ropes at the steepest sections of the Kreuzberge ridge are generally in good condition but inspect before trusting — limestone can be loose in places after freeze-thaw cycles.
  • The traverse from Saxer Lücke to the 7th Kreuzberg summit is ~1.5 h one-way; parties descend by the same route, so mid-ridge turnaround is always an option.
  • As the eastern counterpart to the Lisengrat, the Kreuzberge offers a very different perspective: Rhine valley and Toggenburg views instead of the Bodensee panorama — equally rewarding.

Safety & Hazards

  • T4 means hands needed: The Kreuzberge ridge requires scrambling and use of fixed ropes. Not suitable for those without alpine hiking experience.
  • Limestone extremely slippery when wet: The Alpstein limestone becomes dangerously treacherous in any rain or damp conditions. Abort if weather deteriorates.
  • Thunderstorms: The ridge is a fully exposed lightning conductor. Descend immediately if cumulus clouds develop — aim to be below Saxer Lücke by 13:00 on uncertain days.
  • Rockfall: Stay alert on the crest — parties above can dislodge stones. Helmet recommended.
  • Route-finding above the tree line: Cairns mark the route; in mist the ridge can be difficult to follow. Download the SwissTopo GPX before departure.
  • Phone reception: Patchy between Seealpsee and the ridge. Download maps offline.
  • Emergency: Rega 1414 or 112. Nearest hut: Berggasthaus Meglisalp (1517 m) to the west.
  • This is a summer route only. Any snow or ice on the ridge elevates the difficulty to T5/T6.
